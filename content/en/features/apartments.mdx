## Defending Your Property: Preventing Raids and Thefts

Owning property comes with substantial risk. In San Diego Border Roleplay, other players and corrupt factions actively hunt for lucrative apartment setups to breach and plunder.

```
[Exterior Surveillance] ➔ [Reinforced Door Lock] ➔ [Chokepoint Defense] ➔ [Interior Safe Room]
```

### Understanding Break-In Mechanics
Thieves and raiders utilize in-game tools—such as tablets, police database lookups, and specialized breaching tools—to find property owners and force their way inside. If someone tracks your name through terminal records, your apartment could become a primary target.

| Defense Layer | Implementation Method | Effectiveness | Countered By |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Door Security** | Upgraded electronic locks / codes | High against casual looters | Breaching charges / keyless scripts |
| **Interior Obstacles** | Furniture barricades at the doorway | Moderate (Slows down pushers) | Explosives / tactical entry |
| **OpSec (Discretion)** | Avoiding entering while being tailed | Very High | Direct surveillance |
| **Active Sentry** | Leaving an armed squad member inside | Highest | Coordinated multi-man raids |

### Essential Security Habits
To keep your investment safe, adopt these basic security rules:

* **Always Lock the Door:** Never leave an apartment door ajar, even for a few seconds while grabbing items outside.
* **Watch Your Six:** Before entering your complex, ensure no suspicious players are tailing you to discover your room number.
* **Clear the Tablet Trail:** Be cautious about running high-profile criminal warrants that make your name searchable on municipal lookup tablets.

While **San Diego Roleplay apartments** are ideal for compact printer setups and fast mobility, full houses offer unique advantages for advanced players and syndicates.
