## Checkpoint Operations and Search Protocols

The entry checkpoint is the central hub of activity for law enforcement. Civilian drivers approach the inspection gates, where officers must determine whether a vehicle is clear to pass or requires a secondary search.

                          ▼               ▼
                   [ Clean Search ]   [ Contraband Found ]
                          │               │
                          ▼               ▼
                    [ Released ]      [ Suspect Detained ]

Community reports highlight that smugglers often use clever deception—such as traveling in convoys or using stock vehicles—to blend into heavy traffic. Effective officers follow standard operating procedures to avoid bottlenecks while preventing illegal items from slipping through.

### Standard Checkpoint Inspection Stages

| Step | Action Required | What to Look For | Recommended Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Primary Stop** | Halt vehicle at the gate barrier | Missing plates, expired paperwork, nervous driver behavior | Request player identification and vehicle registration |
| **2. Initial Interview** | Ask driver's destination and purpose | Hesitation, inconsistent stories, non-compliance | Move to secondary lane if answers seem suspicious |
| **3. Trunk & Cabin Search** | Interact with vehicle storage compartments | Hidden firearms, narcotics, prohibited cargo | Call backup before opening compartments on flagged cars |
| **4. Clearance or Detention** | Issue pass ticket or place suspect in custody | Attempted fleeing, weapon drawing, lawful compliance | Open barrier for clean drivers; initiate code arrest for contraband |

When conducting searches, always position your character at an angle where you cannot be easily hit if the driver decides to accelerate abruptly.

## Tactical Communication and Radio Codes

Clear communication separates an unorganized police response from an effective tactical unit. When pursuing fleeing suspects or calling for secondary inspection backup, utilizing standard roleplay radio protocol keeps channels clear and concise.

### Essential Radio Signals

* **10-4:** Acknowledged / Message received.
* **10-8:** In service and available for calls.
* **10-20:** Current location.
* **10-23:** Arrived on scene.
* **10-38:** Conducting a traffic stop / checkpoint inspection.
* **10-80:** Vehicle pursuit in progress.
* **10-99:** Officer in distress / Urgent assistance required.

Whenever you initiate a pursuit as **San Diego Roleplay police**, state your location, vehicle description, and direction of travel immediately. For instance: *"Unit 2 is 10-80 westbound on Main Avenue, suspect in a black sports coupe, speeds in excess of 90 mph."*

## Rules of Engagement and Moderation Guidelines

Roleplaying as a police officer comes with strict behavioral guidelines. Abuse of power, random arresting (RDA), or random deathmatching (RDM) undermines server balance and can lead to immediate moderation actions by game administrators.

### Core Roleplay Standards

1. **Reasonable Suspicion:** Never pull over or search a vehicle without legitimate roleplay cause (e.g., traffic violation, checkpoint mandate, erratic driving, or matching BOLO descriptions).
2. **Escalation of Force:** Always exhaust verbal commands and non-lethal equipment before discharging a firearm. Firearms are restricted to scenarios where player life is directly threatened.
3. **No Exploiting or Auto-Farming:** Using third-party scripts to bypass checkpoint processing or automatically collect duty pay results in permanent platform bans.
4. **Fair Roleplay Interactions:** Give suspects time to respond in chat or through voice channels. Avoid instantly handcuffing players without providing instructions or a reason for the stop.

## Tips to Master the San Diego Roleplay Police Role

Player experience shows that the most successful officers focus on situational awareness and patience rather than aggressive driving. Keep the following operational tips in mind:

* **Always Check the Blind Spots:** Smugglers often approach the checkpoint in lanes with fewer officers. Position yourself near unmanned lanes to prevent unauthorized pass-throughs.
* **Maintain Vehicle Distance:** During high-speed pursuits, maintain a two-car buffer. Tailgating fleeing suspects often leads to collisions that disable your own cruiser.
* **Coordinate Spike Strip Deployments:** Never place spike strips directly in front of you while standing in an active lane. Deploy them ahead of a suspect's path and take cover behind your cruiser.
* **Partner Up on Shifts:** Running a checkpoint gate with a partner cuts inspection times in half. One officer can conduct the interview while the second runs vehicle searches.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I become a police officer in San Diego Roleplay?
To play as law enforcement, enter the team selection menu upon joining the game and sign up as an agent or patrol officer. Certain advanced divisions or tactical fleets may require joining the official community group or ranking up through active duty time.

### What should I do if a driver runs through the San Diego Roleplay police checkpoint?
If a driver breaches the barrier without clearance, do not immediately fire your weapon. Announce a 10-80 over the radio, spawn your interceptor cruiser, and deploy pursuit tactics or spike strips ahead of the suspect's route.

### Can civilians carry firearms in San Diego Roleplay?
Civilians may carry legal items depending on state licensing laws established in the server rules. However, carrying concealed prohibited weapons or unregistered contraband into the city without authorization will lead to detention by **San Diego Roleplay police**.

### What happens if I abuse police authority in the game?
Abusing tools, arresting innocent civilians without valid cause, or exploiting mechanics violates community guidelines. Server administrators actively moderate experiences, and repeated infractions will result in temporary or permanent kicks and bans.
